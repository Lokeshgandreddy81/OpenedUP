Chapter 3 · Water in motion

The Water Cycle

Water continually moves between Earth’s surface and the atmosphere. This movement is called the water cycle, a system powered primarily by energy from the Sun and by gravity.

Evaporation and condensation

When sunlight warms water in oceans, lakes, and soil, some of it changes from liquid into water vapor. The vapor rises into the atmosphere. As it cools, it condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds.

Cloud droplets combine and grow. When they become heavy enough, water returns to the surface as precipitation. Some flows over land into rivers and lakes; some seeps into the ground and replenishes groundwater.

A continuous system

These processes happen at the same time across the planet. A water molecule may move through the atmosphere, fall as rain, and later return to the ocean. The cycle connects the atmosphere, land, and bodies of water.

Local conditions affect how quickly water moves through each part of the cycle. Temperature, wind, vegetation, and the shape of the land all influence evaporation, cloud formation, and runoff.
